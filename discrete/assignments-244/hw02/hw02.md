> Prove using the notion without loss of generality that $5x + 5y$ is an odd integer when $x$ and $y$ are
integers of opposite parity (one is even and another is odd).


Without loss of generality, assume that $x$ is even and $y$ is odd.

Then, by definition, there exist integers $m$ and $n$ such that $x = 2m$ and $y = 2n + 1$.

Substitute $x = 2m$ and $y = 2n + 1$ into the equation $5x + 5y$:

$5(2m) + 5(2n + 1) = 10m + 10n + 5 = 2(5m + 5n + 2) + 1$

Since integers are closed under addition and multiplication, $5m + 5n + 2$ is an integer and $2(5m + 5n + 2)$ is even. Thus, $2(5m + 5n + 2) + 1$ is odd.

Therefore, $5x + 5y$ is an odd integer when $x$ and $y$ are integers of opposite parity. $\blacksquare$
