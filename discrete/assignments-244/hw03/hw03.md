## Question 3

> Prove that you can use dominoes to tile a rectangular checkerboard with an even number of
squares.


First, we show that if a checkerboard is rectangular with an even number of squares, then at least one side length is even.

The area of a rectangle is given by the formula $area = length \times width$.

For the sake of contrapositive, assume that both the length and width are odd. 

Then there exists integers $m$ and $n$ such that $length = 2m + 1$ and $width = 2n + 1$.

Then the area of the rectangle is given by $area = (2m + 1)(2n + 1) = 4mn + 2m + 2n + 1 = 2(2mn + m + n) + 1$.

Since integers are closed under addition and multiplication, $2mn + m + n$ is an integer. Therefore $2(2mn + m + n)$ is even and thus $2(2mn + m + n) + 1$ is odd. 

Therefore, if both the length and width are odd, the area of the rectangle is odd. By contrapositive, if the area of the rectangle is even, then at least one side length is even.

---

Since at least one side is even, at least one side is divisible by 2.

Thus, we can take the even side and divide it into rows of height 2. Since these rows are of height 2, we can place dominoes vertically in each column of each row.

Therefore, if the board is rectangular with an even number of squares, we can tile the board with dominoes.  $\blacksquare$


## Question 4

> Show that by removing two white squares and two black squares from an 8 × 8 checkerboard
(assume only black and white colors are used) you can make it impossible to tile the remaining
squares using dominoes.


If we remove two white squares and two black squares, we can isolate a single square such that it is effectively removed from the board. Since the rules state that dominoes must cover two squares to be placed, if any given tile has no adjacent tiles, it cannot be covered by a domino. 


Thus, if we remove two white squares in, for example, positions (2, 2) and (2, 4) and two black squares in positions (1, 2) and (2, 1), then the white tile in position (1, 1) will have no adjacent tile and thus cannot be covered by a domino. At that point the board will essentially have 59 squares (64 intially, 60 after removing 2 white and 2 black, and 59 considering the unreachable tile at position (1, 1)). 


Since 59 is an odd number, it is impossible to tile the board with dominoes, given that they are 1x2 in size and therefore require an even number of squares to tile.


Therefore, by removing two white squares and two black squares from an 8x8 checkerboard, we can make it impossible to tile the remaining squares using dominoes.  $\blacksquare$

## Question 11

> Show that the sequence {$a_n$} is a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = -3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2}$ if

#### a) $a_n = 0$.


If $a_n = 0$, then $-3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2} = -3(0) + 4(0) = 0 = a_n$.

Therefore, $a_n = 0$ is a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = -3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2}$.  $\blacksquare$

#### b) $a_n = 1$.

If $a_n = 1$, then $-3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2} = -3(1) + 4(1) = 1 = a_n$.

Therefore, $a_n = 1$ is a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = -3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2}$.  $\blacksquare$

#### c) $a_n = (-4)^n$.

If $a_n = (-4)^n$, then $-3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2} = -3(-4)^{n-1} + 4(-4)^{n-2} =$

$\frac{-3}{-4} \cdot (-4)^n + \frac{4}{16} \cdot (-4)^n = \frac{3}{4} \cdot (-4)^n + \frac{1}{4} \cdot (-4)^n = (-4)^n$ = $a_n$.

Therefore, $a_n = (-4)^n$ is a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = -3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2}$.  $\blacksquare$

#### d) $a_n = 2(-4)^n + 3$.

If $a_n = 2(-4)^n + 3$, then $-3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2} = -3(2(-4)^{n-1} + 3) + 4(2(-4)^{n-2} + 3) =$

$(\frac{-6}{-4} \cdot (-4)^n - 9) + (\frac{8}{16} \cdot (-4)^n + 12) = \frac{6}{4} \cdot (-4)^n - 9 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot (-4)^n + 12 = 2(-4)^n + 3 = a_n$.

Therefore, $a_n = 2(-4)^n + 3$ is a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = -3a_{n-1} + 4a_{n-2}$.  $\blacksquare$

## Question 12

> Is the sequence {$a_n$} a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = 8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2}$ if


#### a) $a_n = 0$.

If $a_n = 0$, then $8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2} = 8(0) - 16(0) = 0 = a_n$.

Therefore, $a_n = 0$ **is** a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = 8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2}$.  $\blacksquare$

#### b) $a_n = 1$.

If $a_n = 1$, then $8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2} = 8(1) - 16(1) = 8 - 16 = -8 \neq a_n$.

Therefore, $a_n = 1$ **is not** a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = 8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2}$.  $\blacksquare$

#### c) $a_n = 2^n$.

If $a_n = 2^n$, then $8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2} = 8(2^{n-1}) - 16(2^{n-2}) = 4 \cdot 2^n - 4 \cdot 2^n = 0 \neq a_n$.

Therefore, $a_n = 2^n$ **is not** a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = 8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2}$.  $\blacksquare$

#### d) $a_n = 4^n$.

If $a_n = 4^n$, then $8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2} = 8(4^{n-1}) - 16(4^{n-2}) = \frac{8}{4} \cdot 4^n - \frac{16}{16} \cdot 4^n = 2 \cdot 4^n - 4^n = 4^n = a_n$.

Therefore, $a_n = 4^n$ **is** a solution of the recurrence relation $a_n = 8a_{n-1} - 16a_{n-2}$.  $\blacksquare$
